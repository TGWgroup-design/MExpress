CAR HIRE PARTNER PAYMENT INTEGRATION

Processes and policies | Nov 5, 2026

Prepared by TGW Group for M.Express

Purpose and scope

This framework extends the existing M.Express deposit payout and reimbursement model to vehicle hire deposits specifically: bookings with Telegenix, Europcar, Hertz, First Car Rental and similar asset partners, where a refundable security or damage deposit is held against the hire.

It covers the roles of the three parties, the payment and data integration between M.Express and each car hire company, the policies for onboarding a hire company as a partner, and the controls that keep the client advance, the final approved refund and the reimbursement from M.Express in step with each other.

It does not cover the hire agreement itself, vehicle insurance, or the deposit amount set by the hire company – those stay the car hire company’s terms. It assumes the legal and regulatory classification work in the existing South Africa requirements and compliance checklist runs in parallel, since this product sits inside that same classification question.

Key difference from the base model: a vehicle hire deposit is frequently not refunded in full. Fuel, tolls, e-tags and traffic fines (which can arrive weeks after return) routinely reduce the amount the hire company ultimately approves. M.Express often advances against a figure that is not yet final – this framework exists to manage that gap.

Commercial model for vehicle hire deposits

The fee and payout mechanics follow the standard M.Express model: the client pays a 5% fee, and the car hire company reimburses the full amount paid out, with no extra fee to the partner.

The difference in vehicle hire is when the approved amount is known. A generic refund is a fixed, pre-confirmed figure. A vehicle hire deposit clears in two stages: a provisional approval at return (visible damage only – tolls, e-tags and fines can still arrive later), then a final approval 14-30 days later once charges are reconciled.

The full provisional model is the one that gives clients the speed they expect, but it also creates a receivable against the client, not just the partner. Every claim record carries both the provisional and the final amount, so any shortfall is traceable to a specific claim and recoverable.

Payment and data integration

Two integration tiers, so a large partner like Telegenix and a small independent hire company can both connect.

Integration tierHow the partner connectsTypical partner
API / webhookPartner’s reservation system posts claims to the M.Express claims API; M.Express pushes status webhooks (approved, paid, reimbursement due, overdue) back to the partnerNational/franchise chains (Telegenix, Europcar, Hertz, First Car Rental)
Partner portalPartner staff log in to submit claims, upload return and damage documentation, and view amounts due, manuallyIndependent and regional hire companies

Data exchanged per claim, at minimum:

  • Client identity and bank details, for payout and verification
  • Deposit reference and hire agreement number
  • Provisional approved amount at return, and final approved amount once charges are reconciled
  • Supporting documents: hire agreement, return inspection report, any damage or fine evidence
  • Claim status and the payment and reimbursement references tied to it

Settlement runs through the partner’s and M.Express’s own business bank accounts via EFT; no funds are held on behalf of either party outside a claim’s own payout and reimbursement entries – consistent with paying its own financing obligations rather than operating a payment system for others, the distinction the existing compliance checklist flags for the bank and SARB assessment.

New partners start on the final-approval advance model and a conservative exposure limit. A partner earns the full provisional advance model and a higher limit only after a track record of on-time reimbursement – for example 90 days and 20+ settled claims with no overdue reimbursement.

Risk, exposure and fraud controls

These extend the operational controls already set out in the existing compliance checklist with controls specific to having many hire-company partners and a provisional/final amount gap.

  • Per-partner exposure limit – total outstanding advances to any one car hire company capped and monitored in real time; new claims from a partner at its limit are held, not paid.
  • Per-client exposure limit – a client cannot have more than one open advance at a time across partners, checked by ID at claim verification.
  • Duplicate-claim check – deposit reference and hire agreement number checked against open and settled claims before payout, to prevent the same deposit being financed twice.
  • Independent bank-detail verification – any change to a client’s or partner’s bank details is verified through a separate channel before the next payout.
  • Separation of duties – the person approving a claim is not the person releasing the payment.
  • Daily reconciliation – every payout and reimbursement matched to its claim; unmatched items escalated the same day.
  • Provisional-to-final variance monitoring – claims where the final approved amount comes in materially lower than the provisional amount are flagged for review, both as a client shortfall-recovery case and as a signal to reassess that partner’s or claim type’s risk.
  • Working capital buffer – sized to cover partners’ average reimbursement cycle plus a stress margin, so one partner’s late reimbursement doesn’t stall payouts on other partners’ claims.
M.EXPRESS

Deductions, disputes and partial refunds

This is the step that doesn’t exist in a generic deposit refund.

  1. At vehicle return, the hire company confirms a provisional approved amount. If M.Express has advanced against this figure (full provisional model), that advance is a client receivable, not yet a closed claim.
  2. The hire company finalises the amount – typically 14-30 days later – once tolls, e-tags and fines are reconciled; this is the final approved amount.
  3. If final equals provisional, the reimbursement pack is issued for the full amount and the claim proceeds to settlement as normal.
  4. If final is lower than provisional, the hire company’s claim pack must itemise the deductions. M.Express issues the client a shortfall notice referencing the claim, with the deduction breakdown attached, and recovers the shortfall under the client agreement’s clawback terms.
  5. If the client disputes a deduction, the dispute is routed to the hire company – the party that applied the charge – not resolved by M.Express. M.Express pauses shortfall recovery on the disputed portion only, and the claim stays open until the hire company confirms or reverses it.
  6. If the hire company’s own claim is disputed or incomplete, it is held at “Awaiting reimbursement” and does not count against that partner’s exposure limit as settled.

The reimbursement pack (proof of payment, client acknowledgment, itemised final amount) shows provisional, deductions and final figures side by side – the same discipline the base system already applies to fee and payout breakdowns.

An overdue reimbursement from one partner doesn’t stop that partner’s other in-flight claims from being honoured to clients. It reduces that partner’s available exposure limit for new claims until resolved, so one slow partner can’t be absorbed by over-exposure elsewhere.
